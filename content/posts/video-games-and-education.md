Will you get smarter through playing **video games**?

- Every person that I know, which has played Video Games in their childhood shows a similar minimum level of intelligence. There is barely no person that played video games and is right out stupid (with the exception for games like Fifa).
- This is especially true for Software Developers. Every good Software Developer I know has played at least some amount of video games early in his life.
- There seems to be an even stronger correlation between the kind of games played and the persons intelligence.
- This led me to the hypothesis, that video games will play an important part in increasing humanities intelligence level.

This blog posts is structured in such a way that I first explore the direct benefits of playing video games. The implications are explored in the next part, followed up by some caveats which …

# Benefits

- Understand the rules of system and learn to navigate this rule space.

- Build up intuition

  - Physical Systems
  - Decision Making

- Naturally builds up curiosity
  - Especially true for history and general knowledge
  - Strategic Video Games like Civilization teach intuition on history

# Implications for the real life

- Highly scalable for research
  - The fundamental principle is simulation
  - We always discover in simulated environments
  - Since empirical research is not always feasible in the real world, we have to build simulated environments. For example, instead of building several drone prototypes, we can have a rapid prototyping phase in a digital environment and focus on the most "vielversprechendsten" designs before building the first real model.
- Kids that play video games might tend to want to build up their own games and virtual worlds, which leads to great software developers.

- The small stuff adds up as well. I remember wanting to mod games like Minecraft, which forced me to engage deeper with my computer.

How to get the most out of video games:

- See them as learning opportunities.
- Take a structured learning approach. For example, mechanical Games like Fighting Games are great to focus on your learning skills, on how to repeat stuff, how to get better etc

# Caveats

Some caveats. People that only played Gameplay heavy games, which focus purely on the motorical aspect will not teach you as much as other Games. For example Fighting Games are great for many reasons which I already mentioned above, but they will not grow you social or emotional intelligence. They do improve decision making

This made me wonder, whether a lot of schools curriculum could be replaced or at least supplemented by video games.
